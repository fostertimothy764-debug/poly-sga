# Product

## Register

product

## Users

**Students (~1,500, Baltimore Polytechnic Institute, grades 9–12).** Visit on a phone, between classes or at lunch, in a noisy hallway under bright fluorescent light. Sessions are short — most under a minute. They are checking what's happening this week, whether anything affects their class year specifically, and whether SGA is doing anything worth caring about. They are anonymous; no account, no PII. They are high-schoolers, not children: they read adult writing and resent being talked down to.

**Officers (~25, SGA leadership + class reps + club leads).** Post from a laptop after meetings, often in batches. They want to publish quickly to the right audience without thinking about the schema. Their credibility with the student body depends on the site looking like the work of people who know what they're doing.

## Product Purpose

A weekly source of truth for what student government at Poly is actually doing — announcements, events, ideas the student body is voting on, who's on the team, what clubs exist. The product replaces fragmented Instagram stories, GroupMe links, and hallway posters with one place that's worth checking.

Success is **legitimacy**. A student who lands on the home page should come away with the sense that SGA is a real organization doing real work, not a club running a Linktree. The metric isn't engagement, time-on-site, or vote counts. It's whether the site changes how seriously students take their student government.

## Brand Personality

Warm, confident, published. Three-word personality: **neighborhood, considered, grown-up.**

- Voice is the school paper, not the morning announcements. Plain language, full sentences, dry where appropriate. No exclamation marks by default. No emoji in body copy.
- Treats students as adults. Names class years as "Juniors" / "Class of '28", not "kiddos" or "y'all".
- Warmth comes from typography, real photography, and pacing — not from rounded mascots or pastel gradients.
- Officers' names appear next to their posts. Authorship signals accountability and craft.

## Anti-references

- **LMS portals (Schoology, Blackboard, PowerSchool).** Gray, dense, 2010-era. The default that this site exists to refuse.
- **AI-startup SaaS landings.** Gradient text, hero-metric cards, three-up identical feature grids, "Built with [stack]" badges. Never.
- **Kids' / mascot apps (Duolingo, ClassDojo).** Cartoon characters, oversized rounded buttons, gamified streaks. Patronizing to high-schoolers.
- **Algorithmic feeds (Instagram, TikTok).** Infinite scroll, engagement-optimized layouts, like counts in the chrome. Wrong job — students are here to *learn what's happening*, not to be entertained.

## Design Principles

1. **Legitimacy through craft.** Every detail — type, spacing, photo treatment, copy — should signal "made by people who care." That signal is the entire product strategy.
2. **Editorial pacing, not app density.** Pages read like a magazine spread: a clear lead, room to breathe, photos that earn their place. Not a dashboard. Not a feed.
3. **Real over decorative.** Real names, real photos, real dates, real vote counts. Never lorem-ipsum-shaped illustrations, stock icons standing in for content, or filler cards.
4. **Mobile is the product, laptop is the workshop.** Public surfaces (home, events, ideas, scoop) are designed for a thumb in a hallway. Admin surfaces (dashboard, post editor) are designed for a laptop on a desk. Don't compromise either by trying to be one layout.
5. **Class year is identity, not segmentation.** A senior should feel the site knows they're a senior — through copy, color accent, and prioritization — without it feeling like a filter bubble. Schoolwide content always reads as schoolwide.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Pragmatic. No formal WCAG target, but the floor:

- Body text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 against page background.
- All photos and avatars carry alt text or are marked decorative.
- Full keyboard navigation through nav, voting, and forms — no mouse-only interactions.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected: any motion beyond opacity/transform fades is gated behind the media query.
- Color is never the sole carrier of meaning (status, audience, class year always pair with text or icon).
